how is it different or can we make it different from google homes or apple

Good question to nail down before Phase 2, since a reviewer’s first challenge will be “isn’t this just Google Home?” Here’s the direct comparison and where you differ.

What Google Home and Apple Home already do
Both are permission-based: an admin/owner decides what each person can access, and the restricted person sees a smaller version of the same app.

Google Home: Admin vs. Member roles; Members can be given or denied Activity/Settings access; children under 13 get Activity/Settings off by default.
Apple Home: Resident (full access) vs. Guest (limited, schedule-based, security devices only); owner toggles remote access and add/edit permissions.
Neither adapts the interface itself to who’s using it, and neither explains a restriction or lets someone ask for access from inside the app.

Where you can genuinely differ
Your interviews already point to this, even from just three people: Ramz has no trouble, Murium struggles to navigate, and Ayun needs restriction. That’s not really a permissions problem, it’s a complexity and communication problem. So your differentiation is:

Google Home / Apple Home	Your project
What changes per user	Access rights only	Access rights and the interface itself (layout, number of options, language)
When something is blocked	Silent, or a generic “no permission” message	Plain-language explanation of what’s locked and who controls it
What the restricted user can do about it	Nothing in-app; ask the admin outside the system	One-tap request sent to the admin, with approve options (once, temporarily, always)
Elder-specific design	Same app, fewer buttons	Deliberately simplified view: large touch targets, minimal choices, familiar language, matching Murium’s actual difficulty
Underlying goal	Control and security	Reducing dependence on the admin while keeping restrictions understandable and non-stigmatizing
How to phrase this as your contribution
“Existing platforms like Google Home and Apple Home restrict access but keep the interface constant across users. Our project instead adapts both the interface and the interaction: older adults get a simplified, low-choice view suited to their comfort level, children get restrictions that explain themselves, and any restricted user can request access without needing to find the admin outside the app.”

That’s a defensible, novel angle grounded in your own data, since Murium’s difficulty is exactly the kind of finding that justifies “same app, fewer buttons” isn’t enough.

One thing to add to your next interview round: ask Murium and Ayun (or a parent for Ayun) directly, “If something is locked, would you want to know why, and would you want an easy way to ask for it?” That turns your design idea from your assumption into evidence.
